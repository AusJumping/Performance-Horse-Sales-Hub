Deposits:
There is no charge or need to pay a deposit for the first viewing, UNLESS a potential buyer wishes to ‘hold’ a horse and prevent a sale to someone else.
A 10% deposit (or $1000 - whichever is higher) must be paid to ‘hold’ the horse for a second viewing or vet check.
Deposits are paid directly to the horse’s current owner.
The point of a deposit:
From a seller’s point of view: a deposit is used to minimise time wasters, compensate for risk and time spent vet checking and to compensate for lost sales and/or delays to the selling process.
From the potential buyer’s point of view: a deposit is used to hold the horse, so that it is not ‘sold from under’ them. 
If a deposit was fully refundable, there would be no point to having one. 
Deposits will usually be refunded if:
the horse does not behave, as advertised, at the viewing;
known issues are not disclosed; and/ or
vet report/ x-rays is/are sent to the seller, stating that the horse is lame; ‘not fit for purpose’; or considered a ‘moderate’ or ‘high’ risk for advertised purpose.
It is of vital importance that you make sure you receive, in writing, the terms of a deposit payment and conditions regarding deposit refunds, from the seller, prior to paying a deposit
