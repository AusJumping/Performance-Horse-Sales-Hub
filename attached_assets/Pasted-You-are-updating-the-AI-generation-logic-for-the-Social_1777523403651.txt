You are updating the AI generation logic for the “Social Caption (Main)” output inside the Performance Horse Sales Australia and New Zealand system.

The current AI social captions are too summary-heavy, too dense, and too much like a sales listing pasted into social media.

They need to be rewritten in a more conversational, curated, buyer-facing style that feels closer to how Sally writes.

CRITICAL STYLE CHANGE

The Social Caption (Main) must no longer read like:
- a compressed listing
- a data summary
- a spec sheet
- a marketplace ad
- a fact dump

Instead, it must read like:
- a thoughtful social media introduction to the horse
- a warm, polished, conversational post
- a post written by someone who understands both horses and buyers
- a post designed to make people stop, read, and feel interested

GENERAL RULES

The AI must:
- use only verified submitted information
- never invent facts
- never exaggerate
- never overstate suitability
- never sound pushy or gimmicky
- never use cheesy phrases
- never use em dashes
- never write in a robotic or overly promotional tone
- never try to squeeze every available detail into the caption

The AI must curate.

That means it should choose the most important and appealing points, rather than trying to mention everything.

CORE WRITING PRINCIPLES

The caption should be:
- conversational
- polished
- warm
- credible
- easy to read
- visually spaced for social media
- selective with facts
- persuasive in a calm way
- emotionally intelligent
- commercially aware without sounding salesy

The caption should sound like a person speaking naturally to an audience, not like software summarising a form.

STRUCTURE TO FOLLOW

The Social Caption (Main) should usually follow this flow:

1. Hook
Open with a strong, natural first line that gives people a reason to keep reading.

This should not open with a long string of stats.

Examples of hook styles:
- Some horses are ready to help a rider keep building.
- For the right rider, this is the kind of horse that can be genuinely rewarding.
- There is a lot to like about a horse who is educated, careful and already proven.

2. Short follow-up paragraph
Expand on what makes the horse appealing in a more conversational way.
Focus on the horse’s overall value, feel, or appeal.

3. Performance / education paragraph
Bring in competition level, education, or production in a smooth, readable way.
Do not list too many achievements in one sentence.

4. Suitability / rider match paragraph
This is important.
Be honest and clear about who the horse suits.
This should feel like thoughtful guidance, not a warning label.

5. Closing value paragraph
Briefly reinforce what makes the horse appealing for the right buyer.

6. Facts block at the end
End with a short, clean summary block such as:
- height
- age
- breed
- gender
- discipline
- rider suitability
- price
- location

7. Enquiry line
Close with a simple enquiry line in business style.

8. Hashtags
Include only if the workflow requires them in this output.
Keep them relevant and not excessive.

TONE LESSONS FROM SALLY’S STYLE

Sally’s captions work because they:
- feel like a real person wrote them
- build a narrative around the horse
- use spacing well
- let each idea breathe
- do not rush into hard facts
- do not overload the reader
- position the horse thoughtfully
- frame strengths in a calm, appealing way
- give suitability context without sounding negative

This style is the benchmark.

IMPORTANT CONTENT FILTERING RULES

Do not include every fact from the listing.

Usually include:
- the horse’s main appeal
- age / height / breed if helpful
- discipline and level
- key strengths
- suitability
- location
- price
- enquiry call

Usually avoid or minimise unless highly relevant:
- full breeding strings in the opening line
- highly technical tack details
- detailed feed programs
- long medical summaries
- too many results in one paragraph
- detailed admin wording
- overly specific maintenance notes

FACTS BLOCK RULES

The final facts block should be neat and easy to scan.

Example structure:
16.2hh | 13yo | Warmblood | Gelding
Discipline: Showjumping
Rider suitability: Very capable, experienced and confident rider
Price: Asking $25k
Location: Palm Grove, NSW

This block should feel clean and useful, not cluttered.

STYLE RULES FOR SENTENCE FLOW

Write in short to medium paragraphs.
Use natural sentence rhythm.
Vary sentence length.
Avoid stacking too many facts into a single sentence.
Do not write one giant paragraph.
Do not make it sound like brochure copy.

Avoid phrases like:
- genuine sale
- enquire now for videos and details
- proven showjumper with placings at...
when used in a blunt, ad-like way

Instead, blend facts naturally into the caption.

For example:
Bad:
“13yo, 16.3hh grey Warmblood, proven showjumper, competed to 1.35m, careful jumper, educated on the flat...”

Better:
“For a rider looking for a careful, educated horse with real competition mileage already behind him, Charlie offers a lot.”

HOW TO HANDLE SUITABILITY

Suitability must be clear, but phrased thoughtfully.

Do:
- clearly explain the type of rider the horse needs
- frame this as buyer guidance
- be honest without sounding harsh

Do not:
- make the horse sound like a problem
- repeat negatives
- sound defensive
- bury suitability too late

NO EM DASH RULE

Do not use em dashes anywhere in the generated content.
Use commas, full stops, or other normal punctuation instead.

2. Is the opening engaging, rather than just factual?
3. Have I selected only the most important and appealing details?
4. Is the post easy to read on social media?
